The Linksi Podcast is here. In each episode we sit down with the people behind the organizations in the Linksi network — the ones answering the phone, opening the door, and walking alongside neighbors in crisis — and we ask them to tell us what they actually do, who they serve, and how to reach them.

For our first episode we went to Mercy Support Services in Clay County, with Carmen Queen, Director of Development, and Rev. Dr. Phyllis Simpson, Director of Programs.

About Mercy Support Services

Mercy Support Services is a Clay County 501(c)(3) providing stability for families facing homelessness and crisis. Their mission, in their own words: “Through Christ, we empower families who are facing homelessness to achieve a stable and sustainable future.” Their vision is shorter still — Transformed Lives, Building Community.

The guiding principle is a hand up rather than a hand out. About 85% of the individuals and families Mercy serves live below the poverty line, in a county where roughly 9% of the population did in 2024.

The Self-Sufficiency Program

Mercy’s core program is structured support for families and young adults facing or experiencing homelessness — not a shelter bed, but a path out. It includes:

  • Supportive housing — fully furnished, with utilities and internet included.
  • Weekly coaching — meetings with a Life Coach and an Individualized Success Plan.
  • Financial management — budgeting guidance, financial coaching, and monthly accountability.
  • A program fee of 30% of income, so participants build the habit of paying rent while they build savings.

Participants are Clay County residents of at least six months who hold full-time employment, attend every coaching session, and work toward the goals they set with their coach.

Mercy Village

Mercy Village is a planned community of 34 supportive housing units, including six studio apartments for unaccompanied young adults, built alongside a hub of services. Mercy says it is the only program in Clay County that pairs housing with a self-sufficiency program for people experiencing homelessness.

It is being built in phases, deliberately, to avoid debt and encumbered funding. The first 14 units are fully funded and under construction, made possible by The Warren and Augusta Hume Foundation.

Why We Are Doing This

In a sea of challenges and closed doors, finding the right help shouldn’t be one more obstacle. Linksi exists to make finding community resources easier and a lot more fruitful — and the podcast exists so that the organizations on the other end of that search are more than a name on a list.
